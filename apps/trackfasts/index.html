Beginner Friendly · Leangains

16 hours fasting, 8 hours eating. The most popular and sustainable starting schedule. Finish dinner by 8 PM, fast overnight, and enjoy your first meal at 12 PM the next day.

Intermediate · Fat Loss

18 hours fasting, 6 hours eating. For fasters adapted to 16:8 who want enhanced fat oxidation and early autophagy activation. Eating window e.g., 12 PM to 6 PM.

Advanced · Warrior Diet

20 hours fasting, 4 hours eating. A compact 4-hour evening eating window (e.g., 2 PM to 6 PM or 4 PM to 8 PM) for intensive cellular repair and deep fat burning.

What Happens to Your Body During a Fast

TrackFasts maps your elapsed hours to 6 clinically recognized physiological stages, keeping you educated and motivated throughout your fasting journey.

🍽️

Stage 1: Fed State & Nutrient Absorption

0 – 4 Hours

Your body digests your last meal. Blood glucose and insulin levels peak as nutrients are absorbed, utilized for immediate energy, and stored in the liver and muscles as glycogen.

Physiological Marker: High insulin, digestion active, zero fat burning.

📉

Stage 2: Early Fasting & Glycogen Depletion

4 – 8 Hours

Blood sugar normalizes and insulin levels decline steadily. The gastrointestinal tract empties and the body transitions from circulating blood glucose to stored liver glycogen for baseline fuel.

Physiological Marker: Insulin drops, glucagon rises, liver glycogen mobilization begins.

🔥

Stage 3: Fat Burning Transition

8 – 12 Hours

Stored liver glycogen is substantially depleted. The body initiates lipolysis — breaking down stored adipose triglycerides into free fatty acids to fuel muscle and organ tissue.

Physiological Marker: Accelerated lipolysis, free fatty acids enter bloodstream.

⚡

Stage 4: Ketosis & Mental Clarity

12 – 16 Hours

The liver converts fatty acids into ketone bodies (beta-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate). Ketones cross the blood-brain barrier, providing steady, clean energy that enhances mental focus and curbs hunger.

Physiological Marker: Elevated serum ketones (0.5+ mmol/L), peak fat oxidation rate.

✨

Stage 5: Autophagy Beginning

16 – 24 Hours

Low insulin and amino acid deprivation trigger cellular autophagy (Nobel Prize in Medicine, 2016). Cells begin engulfing and recycling damaged mitochondria, misfolded proteins, and cellular junk.

Physiological Marker: mTOR inhibition, AMPK activation, intracellular autophagosome formation.

🌟

Stage 6: Deep Autophagy & Immune Renewal

24+ Hours

Autophagy reaches maximum intensity. Research shows extended fasting stimulates white blood cell recycling and triggers stem cell-based regeneration of healthy immune cells.

Physiological Marker: Systemic inflammation reduction, deep cellular rejuvenation.
